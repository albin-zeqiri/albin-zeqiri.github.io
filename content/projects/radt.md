<h2>About this project</h2>
Reuse-aware design tools (RADTs) in personal fabrication aim to encourage material reuse by reintroducing scrap parts into fabrication designs. They typically take inventory records as input, which are often incomplete or imprecise in practice, yielding unreliable reuse recommendations. Because RADTs are commonly evaluated on reliable inventories, little is known about which breakdowns arise from unreliable records, how they emerge, and how they affect the perception and uptake of reuse. Surveying 20 RADTs from recent literature, we derive an analytical framework that links how inventory data is obtained to the recommendation breakdowns it can produce, and use it to design a CAD study (N = 23) in which a custom FreeCAD plugin introduces these breakdowns against an idealized control.
