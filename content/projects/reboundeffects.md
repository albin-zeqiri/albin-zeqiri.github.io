<h2>Abstract</h2>
Eco-friendly service options (EFSOs) aim to reduce personal carbon emissions, yet their eco-friendly framing may permit increased consumption, weakening their intended impact. Rebound effects of this kind remain underexamined in HCI, including how common eco-feedback approaches shape them. We conducted an online within-subjects experiment (N=75) in a ride-hailing context. Participants completed 10 trials for five conditions (<i>No EFSO, EFSO - Minimal, EFSO - CO<sub>2</sub> Equivalency, EFSO - Gamified, EFSO - Social</i>), yielding 50 choices between walking and ride-hailing for trips ranging from 0.5&nbsp;mi–2.0&nbsp;mi (≈0.80&nbsp;km-3.22&nbsp;km). We measured how these EFSO variants affected ride-hailing uptake relative to a <i>No EFSO</i> baseline. EFSOs lacking explicit eco-feedback metrics increased ride-hailing uptake, and qualitative responses indicate that EFSOs can make convenience-driven choices more permissible. We conclude with implications for designing EFSOs that begin to take rebound effects into account.
