<h2>Abstract</h2>
As Extended Reality (XR) moves toward habitual, everyday use, the home may no longer only host XR applications but also become a deliberate target of XR mediation. We refer to configurations in which the perceived properties of a home are decoupled from its physical construction, allowing a single dwelling to support multiple configurations, as XR Home Virtualizations (XRHVs). To investigate how residents envision their use and identify the characteristics of desirable and undesirable XRHV futures, we conducted co-speculative sessions with 19 residents across diverse dwellings. A thematic analysis revealed three XRHV roles <i>(fixing/recreating/idealizing)</i>, two dwelling types <i>(XRHV-Agnostic/XRHV-Optimized)</i> and four design dimensions <i>(target, scope, visibility, persistence)</i>. Desirability hinged on divergence from the dwelling's physical capabilities and agency over the resulting configuration. Based on these findings, we contribute a framework for analyzing existing and generating novel XRHV concepts, and discuss implications for designing desirable XRHVs and how dwellings may co-evolve with them.
